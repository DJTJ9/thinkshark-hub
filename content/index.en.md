## home.ueber.1
About me

## home.ueber.2
Sport is taken for granted as something valuable, while gaming is still often reduced to its
downsides. Yet both run on the same principles: practice, feedback, motivation and learning by
doing. Coming from sports science, I have spent three years building digital games that use and
amplify that potential. No other medium can make complex relationships and dry material this
tangible, so that they are understood more easily and stay understood. Combined with a motivating
play experience, that is what I consider an ideal learning environment. My focus areas are complex
systems, data analysis, engine tools, workflow optimisation and game-specific AI.
For the past few months I have been deliberately training my programming skills in combination with
AI models, backed by a harness of my own that I have built and steadily refined over many months. The
AI remains a multiplier of my own abilities, but it gives me the chance to think about software
architecture at a higher level.

## home.ueber.3
What drives me

## home.ueber.4
Games are far more than entertainment to me. They combine problem solving and first-hand experience
in a way that traditional forms of teaching often cannot reach. That potential is largest exactly
where a topic is complex or dry: someone who does not just take in information but acts, experiments
and lives with the consequences learns more durably. That is why I want to build games that are fun.
Fun creates intrinsic motivation. It makes people gladly learn even supposedly dull material and
stick with it through hard times.

## home.ueber.5
That conviction has been with me since studying sports science. Training theory and game theory
showed me how much learning and development playful systems can carry. As a course instructor, among
other things in rehabilitation and windsurfing, I have guided people through learning and training.
Today I combine those experiences with my work as a games programmer. What I enjoy most is gameplay
programming, game feel and systems that respond believably to the player. Technically, my focus is
Unity, C#, game design and building engine tools.

## home.ueber.6
Learning games are the topic closest to my heart. My own projects are where those interests meet:
BullseyeQ analyses throw data and derives training drills from it. Desk-Buddy nudges me to move at
my desk. In a shader learning game I set out to build a sandbox where combining effects produces
your own shaders while you learn how they actually work. I enjoy working in a team and communicate
openly. I want to work on things that genuinely move people forward and that they enjoy along the
way.
